01 · The basics

So, what is AI?

AI is software that finds patterns in information to make a useful guess, suggestion, decision or piece of content.

Some systems sort photos or recommend a song. Generative AI creates text, images, audio or code by learning patterns from examples. This can look like understanding, but it isn’t the same as knowing what’s true or feeling what something means.

The OECD describes AI systems as inferring outputs from inputs. OECD guide ↗

03 · What to watch for

Useful, but not always reliable

AI systems learn from information created by people, so they can carry human mistakes and unfairness with them.

01

It can be wrong, very persuasively.

A chatbot may invent a source or mix up a detail. Treat a polished answer as a draft, not proof.

02

Bias can travel at scale.

Training data can reflect unfair patterns. Automated tools may repeat or amplify them, so test outcomes across groups.

03

Privacy is part of the prompt.

Check a tool’s settings before sharing personal, school or work information. Uploaded data may be hard to control.

04

Seeing isn’t always believing.

AI-generated voices or video can help scammers impersonate someone. In a reported Hong Kong case, fraudsters used a deepfake video meeting to convince an employee to transfer about HK$200 million. Read the case report ↗

✿

Pause & check

Before you trust it…

  • Can I verify this elsewhere?
  • Could someone be harmed if it’s wrong?
  • Am I sharing anything private?
  • Should a person make the final call?

NIST’s AI Risk Management Framework highlights reliability, safety, security, privacy and fairness as things teams should manage. Explore the framework ↗

04 · Looking ahead

What might come next?

AI may take on more tasks at work and in everyday life. That could be helpful, but it also raises questions about jobs, security and who is responsible when a system gets something wrong.

AI data centres use a lot of electricity, which puts pressure on local power grids. I think that means energy planning will matter as AI grows. It doesn’t mean blackouts are inevitable, but the infrastructure has to keep up. Reuters on a power-grid plan ↗
